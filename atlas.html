Lenses applied to every chapter

AI lens

The 20 AI assessment questions, an AI Disruption Score (0–10, with confidence, evidence, time horizon and what could change it), and the 20-field AI Impact & Opportunity Brief.

Five-expert gap review

Industry veteran, technology architect, operations director, specialist linguist, industry researcher. Findings are merged into the chapter, then summarised.

Evidence rules

Every statistic carries year, currency, definition, geography, source, method and a confidence tag. Facts, estimates, consensus, opinion, hypothesis and forecast are labelled separately.

Insider knowledge

Each chapter answers: what would a person working in this field know that an outsider would not?

Structural decisions

  • Language education sits inside Volume 4. The 20-volume brief has no education volume, so education and assessment are two chapters in Services, with careers in Volume 16 and AI tutors in Volume 15. If the education material grows past two chapters it will be split into its own volume (logged in the gap register).
  • Linguistics lives in Volume 3. Two practitioner chapters connect each branch of linguistics to a concrete industry problem, rather than a general linguistics course.
  • Internationalization gets four chapters in Volume 9, because the brief flags it as under-explained.
  • Country coverage is regional in Volume 2, with a country profile template applied to each of the 50+ named countries. Countries with no reliable data get proxies, clearly labelled.
  • Verticals are grouped where workflows are identical (for example aviation, maritime and energy share technical-documentation workflows). Every vertical named in the brief still gets its own section with the full 21-point template.
