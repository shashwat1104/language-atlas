Deliverable 6 · Research

Sources, gaps and priorities.

Which topics need the deepest research, which can be learned fast, which need a specialist — and an honest register of what every key source actually measures.

On this page

Research priority tiersSource registerEvidence labels used in the AtlasGap register

Research priority tiers

Tier A · deepest research

Data is thin, contested or changing fast

  • Market size by segment and country (Vol 2)
  • Country-level data outside US, EU, Japan
  • Pay and freelancer rates by geography (Vol 16)
  • AI's real effect on volume, price and margin (Vol 14–15)
  • Data-for-AI market (Vol 15.10)
  • Language-access law by jurisdiction (Vol 17.7)
  • Low-resource language economics (Vol 3)

Tier B · learn quickly

Stable, well documented

  • Core definitions and GILT
  • File formats (published specs)
  • Unicode, BCP 47, CLDR
  • CAT/TM mechanics
  • Translation workflow basics
  • ISO 17100 / 18587 / 5060 scope
  • Subtitle format basics

Tier C · needs a specialist

Errors are costly, rules are local

  • Clinical linguistic validation, eCOA (Vol 12)
  • Medical device regulation: MDR/IVDR, PMDA
  • Sworn translation and apostille by country
  • Court and medical interpreting rules
  • Broadcast captioning regulation
  • Cross-border tax for freelancers
  • Voice rights and AI voice contracts

Evidence labels used in the Atlas

LabelMeaning
Verified factStated by a primary source (standard, filing, official release) and checked
EstimateA number produced by a research firm's model or survey
ConsensusBroadly agreed across several independent sources and practitioners
OpinionA named person's or firm's view
HypothesisThe Atlas's own reasoning, not yet tested against data
ForecastA projection about the future, always with who made it and when
