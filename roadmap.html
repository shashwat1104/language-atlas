Deliverable 3 · Learning

From zero to industry expert.

A path from zero to someone who can walk into any localization company, understand how it works, judge an opportunity and build a product for it.

On this page

Why it is ordered this way

  1. Money and people before tools. Beginners usually start with a CAT tool. That teaches buttons, not the business. You first learn who pays, why, and who does the work — then every tool makes sense as a fix for a known problem.
  2. One real workflow, end to end, early. A single small project (a 500-word file, quoted, translated, checked, invoiced) teaches more than ten chapters read in the abstract. Stage 1 ends with one.
  3. Files are the hidden curriculum. Most real failures are file failures: broken placeholders, wrong encoding, lost tags. File literacy comes in Stage 2, before specialisation.
  4. Specialise only after the general model is solid. Media, clinical, legal and gaming each have their own rules. Learned too early, they look like the whole industry.
  5. AI is taught in every stage, not at the end. Each stage includes how AI changes that layer today, so the learner never builds a pre-AI mental model that has to be unlearned.
  6. Expert stage = judgement. The last stage is about evaluating businesses, building products and arguing with evidence — what separates an operator from a well-read outsider.

The five stages

01BeginnerWeeks 1–4 · understand what the industry is and who pays+

Topics

  • What the language industry is; the six sectors (Vol 1 Ch 1)
  • GILT: globalization, internationalization, localization, translation
  • Who buys: enterprise, government, media, healthcare, legal
  • Who supplies: freelancers, LSPs/LSIs, technology platforms
  • The basic translation workflow (TEP: translate–edit–proofread)

Concepts

  • Source and target, language pair, locale
  • Translation memory, fuzzy match, termbase
  • MT, MTPE, LLM translation
  • Per-word pricing, minimum fee, rush fee

Reading

  • Nimdzi 100 (latest edition, free) — the TL;DR and market sections
  • Found in Translation — Nataly Kelly & Jost Zetzsche (2012): readable stories of why translation matters
  • W3C Internationalization "Localization vs. Internationalization" article
  • Slator and MultiLingual news, 15 minutes a day

Listening and video

  • SlatorPod (weekly) and Localization Today (MultiLingual)
  • GALA webinars archive

Tools

  • A free CAT tool: OmegaT or MateCat
  • A free MT engine (DeepL / Google) and one LLM

Exercises

  • Draw the value chain for one real company (e.g. a streaming service localizing into Hindi) from memory, then check it against Ch 1.
  • Collect five LSP websites; list their services and verticals; spot what they all sell.

Project

Do one complete mini-job: take a 500-word English text, write a quote, translate it into a language you know with a CAT tool, run QA, write a delivery note and an invoice.

Assessment and milestone

Pass the Ch 1 quiz. Milestone: you can explain in two minutes how a translation order moves from client to linguist and back, and how everyone in the chain gets paid.

02IntermediateMonths 2–3 · learn how the work is actually produced+

Topics

  • All 12 workflows (Vol 6), especially software and website
  • CAT vs TMS; how TM and termbases work (Vol 7, 9)
  • File formats: XLIFF, TMX, TBX, JSON, PO, SRT, IDML (Vol 8)
  • Internationalization basics: Unicode, UTF-8, locales, plurals (Vol 9)
  • Quality: error typologies, LQA, ISO 17100 basics (Vol 10)

Reading

  • Bert Esselink, A Practical Guide to Localization (2000) — dated tools, timeless logic
  • Translation Tools and Technologies — Rothwell, Moorkens et al. (Routledge, 2023)
  • Unicode and W3C i18n articles; CLDR plural rules chart
  • MQM (themqm.org) error typology

Tools

  • Trados Studio or memoQ trial; Phrase or Crowdin free tier
  • Xbench or Verifika for QA
  • A code editor (VS Code) to open JSON, XML, PO files

Exercises

  • Open the same content as DOCX, XLIFF and JSON. Identify what each stores (text, tags, status, metadata).
  • Break a JSON resource file on purpose (missing placeholder, bad escape); see how the app fails.
  • Score a translation with an MQM-style scorecard; compare your score with a peer's (calibration).

Projects

  • Localize a small open-source app's strings into one language via a TMS and pull request.
  • Build a TM from aligned bilingual documents and measure leverage on a new file.

Milestone

You can take any common file, predict what will break during localization, and set up a project in a CAT/TMS with TM, termbase and QA rules.

03AdvancedMonths 4–6 · run the business and the technology+

Topics

  • LSP operations: departments, KPIs, PM lifecycle, vendor management (Vol 13)
  • Pricing and unit economics; margin maths (Vol 14)
  • Procurement, RFPs, enterprise buyer behaviour
  • Localization engineering: APIs, connectors, CI/CD (Vol 9)
  • AI workflows: MT/LLM selection, QE, human-in-the-loop by risk (Vol 15)

Reading

  • CSA Research and Nimdzi free reports on pricing and buyers
  • ELIS survey reports (free, yearly)
  • TMS vendor API docs (Phrase, Crowdin, Lokalise)
  • Philipp Koehn, Neural Machine Translation (2020)

Tools

  • A business management system demo (Plunet, XTRF or Protemos)
  • Python + an MT/LLM API; COMET for evaluation

Exercises

  • Build a project P&L: 40,000 words, 8 languages, with vendor rates, PM hours and QA; find the break-even price.
  • Answer a mock RFP for a SaaS company.
  • Design an AI workflow that routes content by risk to raw MT, MTPE or full human translation.

Project

Script a pipeline: pull strings from a repository, translate with an LLM using a glossary, run automated checks, push back as a pull request.

Milestone

You can read a localization company's website and guess its business model, cost structure and likely margins — and say which part AI threatens.

05Industry expertMonths 10–12 · judgement, strategy and building+
  • Topics: market sizing and its traps; M&A and valuations; country and corridor strategy; regulation; the AI disruption scorecard; opportunity evaluation (Vol 2, 14, 15, 17, 19, 20).
  • Exercises: critique two market reports side by side; write an investment memo on a language-tech company; score five opportunities with the Opportunity Score.
  • Project: ship one product from the Open-Source Lab (Vol 19) to real users, or write a published analysis that industry people respond to.
  • Assessment: a panel-style review — explain any company's model, defend a forecast, and answer "what would break if AI got 2× better next year?"
  • Milestone: you can speak with a CEO, a localization engineer and a procurement lead in the same day and be useful to each.

Timelines

30 days

Orientation

  • Week 1: Vol 1 Ch 1; glossary terms 1–40
  • Week 2: workflows and the value chain; install a CAT tool
  • Week 3: file basics (DOCX, XLIFF, JSON); one MT/LLM comparison
  • Week 4: the 500-word mini-job, quoted and invoiced

Outcome: Stage 1 milestone.

90 days

Practitioner

  • Month 1: the 30-day plan
  • Month 2: all workflows; TM/termbase setup; QA tools
  • Month 3: i18n basics; open-source app localization project; MQM scoring

Outcome: Stage 2 milestone; a portfolio of two projects.

6 months

Operator

  • Months 1–3: the 90-day plan
  • Month 4: LSP operations, PM lifecycle, vendor management
  • Month 5: pricing, P&L, RFPs
  • Month 6: AI pipeline project; risk-based workflow design

Outcome: Stage 3 milestone.

12 months

Expert

  • Months 1–6: the 6-month plan
  • Months 7–9: one specialist track with capstone
  • Months 10–12: market analysis, investment memo, one shipped product

Outcome: Stage 5 milestone.
