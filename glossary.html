Deliverable 5 · Glossary

The master glossary.

137 terms and growing. Every chapter adds its own. Search, or filter by area.

terms shown

100% matchtechnology
A TM match identical to the new segment's text.
Addressable marketbusiness
The portion of total spending that an outside provider can realistically sell into — smaller than total market size because some work is done in-house.
Agentic localizationai
Workflows where AI agents plan and execute multi-step localization tasks — intake, context gathering, translation, QA, delivery — with human checkpoints.
AI Disruption ScoreAtlas metricai
This Atlas's 0–10 rating of how strongly AI is changing a segment, role or workflow, always with confidence, evidence and time horizon.
Alignmenttechnology
Pairing sentences from existing source and target documents to build a TM.
Analysis reporttechnology
The CAT/TMS report that breaks a file into match bands (new, fuzzy, 100%, repetitions); the basis for quotes.
Apostilleservices
A certificate under the 1961 Hague Convention that authenticates a public document's origin for use in another member country.
ARBApplication Resource Bundlefiles
JSON-based resource format used by Flutter.
ASRautomatic speech recognitiontechnology
Converting speech to text; the first step in automated captioning and speech translation.
Audio description (AD)services
Narration describing visual content for blind and partially sighted audiences.
Back translationservices
Translating a translation back into the source language, by someone who has not seen the original, to check meaning.
BCP 47language tagi18n
The IETF best practice for language tags (e.g. en-GB, sr-Latn-RS), built on ISO 639, ISO 15924 and ISO 3166 codes.
Bidibidirectional texti18n
Mixing right-to-left (Arabic, Hebrew) and left-to-right text; governed by the Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm.
BLEUquality
An early automatic MT metric based on word overlap with a reference translation; now considered weak for judging modern systems.
Business management system (BMS)TBMSbusiness
Software that runs an LSP's back office: quotes, jobs, vendors, invoices, payments. Examples: Plunet, XTRF, Protemos.
Buyer personabusiness
The type of person who purchases a language service — for example a localization manager, a marketing director or a clinical operations lead.
CAT toolcomputer-assisted translationtechnology
Software in which linguists translate segment by segment with translation memory, termbases, QA and MT suggestions. Examples: Trados Studio, memoQ.
Certified translationservices
A translation accompanied by a signed statement of accuracy; requirements vary by country and receiving authority.
CLDRCommon Locale Data Repositoryi18n
Unicode project supplying locale data — date, number, currency formats, plural rules — used by most operating systems and libraries.
COA / eCOA / ePROregulated
Clinical outcome assessments, in paper or electronic form, including patient-reported outcomes; require linguistic validation.
Cognitive debriefingservices
Interviews with target-language patients to confirm they understand a translated questionnaire as intended.
Collationi18n
Language-specific sort order; alphabetical order differs between Swedish, German and Spanish.
COMETquality
A neural metric that predicts human quality judgements; widely used in MT and LLM evaluation.
Community interpretingpublic service interpretingservices
Interpreting that gives people access to public services — health, police, schools, social care.
Concordance searchtechnology
Searching the TM for a word or phrase to see how it was translated before.
Connectortechnology
An integration that moves content automatically between a TMS and a CMS, code repository, design tool or marketing platform.
Consecutive interpretingservices
The speaker pauses and the interpreter renders each section, often using note-taking.
Context match101% / ICE matchtechnology
A 100% match whose surrounding segments also match; usually needs little or no review.
Continuous localizationtechnology
Localizing small changes as they are written, synced with software release cycles, instead of in large batches.
CPScharacters per secondmedia
Subtitle reading-speed measure; platforms set maximums per language and audience.
CROcontract research organisationregulated
A company that runs clinical trials for sponsors; a major buyer of clinical translation.
Data-for-AIai
Commercial spending on datasets and data services used to train, adapt, evaluate and deploy AI; Slator sized it at about USD 9.3bn in 2026.
Directionalitylinguistics
The direction of translation. EN→JA and JA→EN are separate markets with different supply, rates and AI quality.
DITADarwin Information Typing Architecturefiles
OASIS XML standard for modular technical documentation, designed for reuse and translation.
DQFDynamic Quality Frameworkquality
TAUS's quality framework and tooling, harmonised with MQM.
DTPdesktop publishingservices
Rebuilding layout after translation — handling text expansion, fonts, right-to-left scripts and images.
eDiscoveryregulated
Finding and reviewing electronic documents for litigation; multilingual eDiscovery is a legal-language niche.
Error typologyquality
The list of error categories (accuracy, fluency, terminology, style, locale conventions…) used to score a translation.
Forced narrativemedia
Subtitles that appear even when subtitles are off, for foreign dialogue or on-screen text essential to the story.
Full post-editingservices
Post-editing to a quality comparable with human translation.
Functional QAquality
Testing that localized software works: no truncation, crashes, broken links or untranslated UI.
Fuzzy matchtechnology
A TM match that is similar but not identical to the new segment, expressed as a percentage (e.g. 85%).
GILTservices
Globalization, Internationalization, Localization, Translation — the classic four-layer model of the industry.
Globalization (G11n)services
The business strategy and processes for taking products and operations into multiple markets; localization and internationalization are parts of it.
Grapheme clusteri18n
What a user sees as one character, which may be several code points (e.g. a Devanagari syllable or an emoji with modifiers).
Gross marginbusiness
Revenue minus the direct cost of delivering the work (linguists, vendors, direct tools), as a share of revenue.
Hallucinationai
AI output that is fluent but not supported by the source — added, omitted or invented content.
Human-in-the-loop (HITL)ai
A workflow where AI produces output and humans review, correct or approve it at defined points.
ICFinformed consent formregulated
The document clinical-trial participants sign; must be translated and approved for each language.
IDMLInDesign Markup Languagefiles
Zipped XML representation of an InDesign document; the format CAT tools read for DTP work.
Inline tagfiles
Formatting or code marker inside a segment (bold, link) that must survive translation in the right place.
Internationalization (i18n)services
Designing and engineering a product so it can be localized without re-engineering: Unicode text, external strings, locale-aware formatting, flexible layouts.
Interpretinginterpretationservices
Rendering spoken or signed language into another language in real time or near-real time.
ISO 17100quality
International standard for translation service requirements: resources, process (translation plus revision), and project management.
ISO 18587quality
International standard for full post-editing of machine translation output.
ISO 5060quality
2024 ISO guidance on evaluating human, post-edited and raw machine translation output using error types and penalty points.
JSONLJSON Linesfiles
One JSON object per line; the common format for AI training and evaluation datasets.
Language service provider (LSP)translation agencybusiness
A company that sells language services — translation, interpreting, localization and related work — usually by managing a network of freelance linguists.
Language solutions integrator (LSI)Slator termbusiness
Slator's name for companies that combine human linguists, technology and AI into delivered language solutions; broadly what used to be called an LSP.
Language technology platform (LTP)Slator termbusiness
A company whose main product is language software — TMS, MT, speech or AI — sold as a platform or API.
Leveragetechnology
The share of a new project that can be filled from existing TM; drives price discounts.
Light post-editingservices
Post-editing that fixes only errors of meaning so the text is understandable; style is not polished.
Linguistic validationservices
A multi-step process — forward translation, back translation, reconciliation, cognitive debriefing — that proves a translated clinical questionnaire measures the same concept.
Lip-sync dubbingmedia
Dubbing where the new dialogue is fitted to the actors' mouth movements.
LLMlarge language modeltechnology
A general-purpose language model (e.g. GPT, Claude, Gemini) that can translate, adapt, check and generate content from instructions.
Localei18n
A language plus region (and sometimes script) that determines conventions: fr-CA, zh-Hant-TW, hi-IN.
Localization (L10n)services
Adapting a product or content to a specific locale — language, conventions, culture, legal and technical requirements — so it feels made for that market.
Low-resource languageai
A language with little digital text or speech data, so AI systems perform worse in it.
LQAlinguistic quality assurancequality
Evaluating translated content, usually by sampling, against an error typology and pass/fail threshold.
Machine translation (MT)technology
Automatic translation by software. Today mostly neural MT (NMT) or large language models.
Managed servicebusiness
An arrangement where a vendor runs all or part of a client's localization programme, often with on-site or dedicated staff.
MDR / IVDRregulated
EU regulations for medical devices and in-vitro diagnostics, with language requirements for labels and instructions.
Minimum feeminimum chargebusiness
The lowest price charged for any job, covering fixed project-management and admin cost on very small orders.
Model routingai
Choosing which MT engine or LLM handles a piece of content based on language, domain, risk and cost.
MQMMultidimensional Quality Metricsquality
A framework of error types and severities for analytic translation-quality evaluation.
MTPEmachine translation post-editingservices
Human editing of machine-translated output to an agreed quality level. ISO 18587 defines requirements for full post-editing.
Multi-language vendor (MLV)business
An LSP that covers many languages and manages projects end to end; contrasted with single-language vendors.
Neural machine translation (NMT)technology
MT built on neural networks; became the industry standard from around 2016–2017.
NormalizationNFC / NFDi18n
Converting equivalent Unicode sequences into one canonical form so text compares and searches correctly.
OCRoptical character recognitiontechnology
Extracting text from images or scanned documents so it can be translated.
OPIover-the-phone interpretingservices
Remote interpreting by telephone; widely used in healthcare and public services.
Placeholdervariablefiles
A token in a string (e.g. {name}, %s) replaced at runtime. Breaking one breaks the app.
Plural rulesi18n
Languages have different plural categories (Japanese one, English two, Arabic six); CLDR defines them.
PO / POT / MOgettextfiles
GNU gettext formats: POT is the template, PO the translated text, MO the compiled binary loaded by the app.
Pragmaticslinguistics
How meaning depends on context and intent — the reason transcreation exists.
Prime contractorprimebusiness
The vendor that holds the client contract and subcontracts parts of the work to others.
Proofreadingservices
Final examination of the target text for errors before publication; often on the formatted file.
Pseudo-localizationtechnology
Replacing source strings with altered, expanded, accented text to find internationalization bugs before real translation.
Quality estimation (QE)technology
Predicting the quality of a translation without a reference, used to route segments to human review or skip it.
RAGretrieval-augmented generationai
Giving an LLM relevant TM segments, glossary terms and style rules at prompt time so output follows client assets.
Regexregular expressiontechnology
A pattern language used for QA checks, tag protection and bulk edits in localization files.
Registerlinguistics
Level of formality; Japanese keigo and Hindi आप/तुम choices are register decisions.
Repetitiontechnology
A segment that appears more than once in the same project; translated once and propagated.
RESXfiles
.NET XML resource file for UI strings.
Revenue per employeebusiness
Annual revenue divided by headcount; a common efficiency measure. Nimdzi reports it rose sharply among top-100 providers in 2025.
Reviewmonolingual reviewservices
In ISO 17100, a monolingual examination of the target text for suitability to its purpose and domain.
Reviewer calibrationquality
Aligning several reviewers so they score the same text the same way.
Revisionbilingual reviewservices
In ISO 17100, a bilingual examination of the translation against the source by a second qualified linguist.
RFPrequest for proposalbusiness
A formal invitation from a buyer for vendors to bid on a defined scope of work; usually preceded by an RFI (request for information).
RSIremote simultaneous interpretingservices
Simultaneous interpreting delivered through a cloud platform rather than a physical booth.
RTLright-to-lefti18n
Scripts written right to left; requires mirrored layouts, not just translated strings.
Rush feebusiness
A surcharge for delivery faster than the vendor's standard turnaround.
Script adaptationmedia
Rewriting a translated script so it fits timing, lip movement and performance for dubbing.
SDHsubtitles for the deaf and hard of hearingmedia
Subtitles that also convey speaker identity, sound effects and music.
Segmenttechnology
The unit of translation in a CAT tool — usually a sentence, defined by segmentation rules (SRX).
Severityquality
How serious an error is — e.g. minor, major, critical — usually with penalty weights.
Simultaneous interpretingservices
Interpreting while the speaker talks, usually from a booth with headsets; typically done by teams of two or more who rotate.
Single-language vendor (SLV)business
A provider, often in-country, that specialises in one target language and usually works as a subcontractor to MLVs.
Source languageSLlinguistics
The language the content is written in.
Speech-to-speech translationS2STtechnology
Translating spoken input directly or via text into spoken output in another language.
Spottingtimingmedia
Setting the in and out times of each subtitle against the audio and shot changes.
SRTSubRipfiles
Simple subtitle format: index, timecodes and text.
SRXSegmentation Rules eXchangefiles
Standard for describing how text is split into segments.
.strings / .stringsdictfiles
Apple platform string files; .stringsdict holds plural rules.
Sworn translationservices
A translation made by a translator authorised by a court or ministry, carrying legal validity in that jurisdiction (common in civil-law countries).
Target languageTLlinguistics
The language it is translated into.
TBXTermBase eXchangefiles
XML format (ISO 30042) for exchanging terminology.
TEPtranslation, editing, proofreadingservices
The traditional three-step human workflow: one linguist translates, a second edits against the source, a final check proofreads.
Termbase (TB)technology
A database of approved terms per language, with definitions, usage notes and forbidden variants.
Terminology scienceterminologylinguistics
The study of concepts and their designations in specialised domains; the basis of termbase design (ISO 704).
Text expansioni18n
Translated text often runs longer than English (German, Finnish) or needs more height (Thai, Hindi); UIs must allow for it.
Timecodemedia
Frame-accurate time reference (HH:MM:SS:FF); depends on frame rate.
TMStranslation management systemtechnology
Platform that automates the flow of content: connectors to source systems, workflows, assignments, MT, review, delivery.
TMXTranslation Memory eXchangefiles
XML format for moving translation memories between tools.
Transcreationcreative translationservices
Recreating content, usually marketing, in another language so it produces the same effect, even if the words change entirely. Often priced by the hour.
Translationservices
Rendering written content from one language into another while preserving meaning, intent and function.
Translation memory (TM)technology
A database of previously translated segment pairs, reused when the same or similar text recurs.
TTML / IMSCfiles
W3C timed-text XML; IMSC is the profile used widely by streaming and broadcast.
TTStext-to-speechtechnology
Synthesising spoken audio from text; used in synthetic voiceover and AI dubbing.
Unicodei18n
The universal character standard. Version 17.0 (September 2025) encodes 159,801 characters across 172 scripts.
UTF-8i18n
The dominant Unicode encoding on the web; variable-length and ASCII-compatible.
Vendor management (VM)business
The function that recruits, tests, onboards, rates and pays freelance linguists and subcontractors.
Voiceover (UN-style)media
Translated narration laid over the original audio, which remains faintly audible.
VRIvideo remote interpretingservices
Remote interpreting by video link; essential for sign languages remotely.
WebVTTVTTfiles
W3C subtitle and caption format for web video.
Whispered interpretingchuchotageservices
Simultaneous interpreting delivered quietly to one or two listeners without equipment.
XLIFFXML Localization Interchange File Formatfiles
The OASIS standard bilingual exchange format. Versions 1.2 and 2.x; many tools add proprietary flavours (SDLXLIFF, MQXLIFF, MXLIFF).
